Litmus is a free detector of AI written text that says how sure it is

Paste a passage and see whether it reads as written by a person, by an AI model, or by both, and how sure that reading can be. It runs on your own computer or inside your browser, so your text stays with you.

A quick answer, and the evidence behind it

Free

Free for any noncommercial use, with no account and no paid tier. All of its code is published, so anyone can check what it does.

Your text stays with you

The Windows program contains no network code and works with your connection switched off. The browser page reads every text inside the page.

Says how sure it is

When the evidence cannot separate human, AI, and mixed, it says Uncertain and shows which origins remain, with a probability and its interval.

Fast on any computer

On one processor core the program reads about 28,267 tokens a second. It needs no graphics card.

What a reading tells you

  • The verdict: Human, AI, or Mixed when only one origin remains, Uncertain when the evidence leaves two or three, and Unlike reference when the text resembles none of the texts Litmus knows.
  • The strip runs from human (blue) through mixed (purple) to AI (red). The pale band covers every origin still possible, so a wide band means an unsure reading.
  • Where it changes: in a long text, the tint shows which passages lean human and which lean AI.

Short texts are less reliable: a sentence or two carries little evidence, and the band stays wide to show it. This release is an English detector: almost all of the text it learned from was English, it was tested mainly on English text, and other languages are not supported yet.

A reading is evidence about where a text came from, never proof of who wrote it. We recommend not using a reading alone to accuse anyone. What you do with a reading is your own decision.

Three ways to use it, and where your text goes

Two of them never send your text anywhere. The third is for people who choose to give their texts to improve Litmus.

Text stays in the page

In your browser

The page downloads the model once, then reads every text inside your browser. Nothing to install.

What the page fetches
  • litmus_app_bg.wasmthe app
  • model.safetensorsthe model, once
  • config.json, tokenizer.jsonits tables
What it sends
  • Nothing
 Text stays on your computer

The Windows program

One file, no installer, no account, and it works offline. It is faster than the browser page, and its quick check reads text you select in any application.

Download for Windows
Text is sent and kept privately

The contribute site

The one place where a text is sent to a server. With your explicit consent it keeps the text privately, never published, to make later versions of Litmus better.

Tested on texts it never trained on

Litmus is scored on its own held-out test texts and on the test sets of public benchmarks, beside open detectors run on the same texts. Every result reports PR AUC, ROC AUC, and balanced MCC.
