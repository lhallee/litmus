Results

Litmus is tested on texts it never trained on: its own held-out test split, and the test sets of public benchmarks, each beside open detectors run on the same texts. Every result reports PR AUC, ROC AUC, and balanced MCC with a 95% interval.

How the comparison is run

  • The same texts for everyone. Each benchmark set is a seeded sample of 3,000 texts, and every detector scores the same sample. The test split of every benchmark is held out of all of Litmus's training, bank, and calibration data.
  • Every metric, every time. PR AUC and ROC AUC measure how well scores rank AI text above human text at every threshold. Balanced MCC is the Matthews correlation on class-balanced samples at its best threshold. TPR at 1% and 5% FPR is the share of AI text caught when the threshold flags only 1% or 5% of human text, the number that matters most when a false flag is costly.
  • Paired intervals. Each interval is a stratified bootstrap 95% interval, and every detector uses the same draws, so differences between detectors come from the texts, not the resampling.
  • Three tasks. AI against human with mixed left out, AI or mixed against human, and mixed against human. The selector above switches between them.

What these numbers do not mean

  • They are not a promise about your text. A benchmark is a sample of particular sources, models, and years. Text from newer models or in unusual formats can read differently.
  • They are mainly about English. This release is an English detector: almost all of the text it learned from was English, and other languages are not supported yet. The sets here in other languages, such as RAID extra's Czech and German, show how it reads text it does not support.
  • Different protocols are not one ranking. Numbers published elsewhere use other samples, thresholds, and metrics, so they are not comparable with these unless the benchmark, subset, and metric all match.
  • Training on a benchmark's family helps on its test split. Litmus and several open detectors learned from some benchmarks, from their official training splits and, for some open detectors, from the test texts themselves, and those results are marked T. Results on unmarked benchmarks say more about text a detector has never seen.
  • A sample supports its intervals and no more. A difference inside overlapping intervals is not a difference we can claim, and a measured speed holds for the processor it was measured on, which the speed table names.
  • A high score is not proof about a person. Even at a 1% false positive rate, one human text in a hundred is flagged. We recommend not using a reading alone to accuse anyone.
